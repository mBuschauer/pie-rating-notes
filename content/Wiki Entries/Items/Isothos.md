- The most common [[Wiki Entries/History/Ancient Civilizations/Arlun|Arlunite]] technology that divers manage to find in a usable state are Isothos, or magic prosthetics. 
- Something about the way they were constructed or maintained allowed more of them to survive [[Wiki Entries/History/Events/The Flood|The Flood]] than most types of [[Wiki Entries/History/Ancient Civilizations/Arlun|Arlunite]] tech. 
- Due to the [[#Drawbacks]], people who actively destroy perfectly good organs or limbs to implant an Isotho replacement are generally considered to be mentally unstable on some level
- There are 6 main varieties of Isothos, based on what body part they functionally replace or enhance: Head, Eye, Jaw, Torso, Arm, Leg. 
	- They range in size and quality:
		- A “primitive” arm Isotho, for instance, could be something like a hand replacement, while
		- A “divine” arm Isotho could be something like a replacement for an entire arm, shoulder included. 
- Relatively common attachments include
	- Pulse generators that can create small shockwaves
	- Blade-limbs that function like retractable steel patas
	- Grappling hooks
	- Additional knowledge reserves
	- Modifications like claws or webbed digits
	- Short-range rocket propulsion systems
	- Devices that produce energy constructs like shields or melee weapons

### Features:
- Segmented metallic body part replacements that glow and leak a bright crimson energy, clear evidence of their arcane nature. 
- Their shapes are varied, ranging from very close mechanical approximations to actual body parts to slender, more abstract and sleek sculpts. 
- They can expand and compress to fit most humanoids. 
- They seemingly require no external power source, can grant great power, and can be easily customized with modular Isotho attachments. 
### Drawbacks
- Isothos are still old technology and can suffer from issues of consistency. 
- Some Isothos have detonated upon repeated failure, disintegrating their user and everything else within several feet. 
- Using an Isotho for an extended period of time has a provable negative effect on an average person’s lifespan.
